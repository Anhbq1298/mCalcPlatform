mPunchingShear calculation help

How mPunchingShear checks slab–column connections, places shear links and presents the governing result to EN 1992-1-1:2004.

Check summary. The summary shows the selected code profile and combination. Select a check to highlight its perimeter or governing link layer on the plan. When concrete resistance is exceeded and no links are provided, the reported utilisation uses the concrete resistance at the basic control perimeter.

Compare layouts. With links enabled and required in a slab without a drop, compare the orthogonal grid, radial rails and cruciform rails using the current actions and detailing constraints. The table shows link count, tangential spacing, required and effective outer lengths where available, and the governing combination.

Custom layout. When an empty Custom layout is opened from Auto, the rows start with the calculated link positions. Adjusting a row's tangential spacing redistributes that row evenly along its path. Adjusting a radial spacing rebuilds that row and those beyond it. Select a link in the plan to omit it or restore a layer's links.

6 Input: total drop thickness and link arrangement

The drop is entered by its total thickness htot (slab plus drop); the projection below the slab is hH = htot − h and dH = d + hH. Designs saved with the older depth input open with htot = h + hH.

Provided links are entered one row per layer (L1, L2, …): the radial spacing s from the previous layer (L1 from the column face), the bar, the number of legs and the tangential spacing st (link centre to centre along the layer). The number of links per layer follows from st: ceil(u / st) on a complete layer, ceil(length / st) on each part of a layer cut by a free edge or an opening, so no gap exceeds st and longer outer layers get more links. Auto arrangement runs the automatic design and writes one row per layer with st = the largest designed gap rounded down to 5 mm, so the arrangement never has fewer links than the design. With a drop, the arrangement runs straight across the drop edge: layers inside the drop belong to the section with dH, layers beyond it to the section around the drop with d.

Auto design places the links in one of three arrangements, with the first perimeter at s0 = max(0.3d, 0.4d rounded down to 5 mm) from the column face and the radial spacing sr (default 150 mm, also used in Eq. 6.52): orthogonal grid (default; the grid follows the flexural bars chosen under Top flexural reinforcement: links at the bar intersections with sr = st = bar spacing, bars set out symmetric about the column with a bar line at s0 outside each face and a closing bar on the centre line where the middle gap would exceed the spacing; for a column rotated to the bars the grid is laid out around the circumscribed rectangle in the bar directions (the two farthest opposite points each way) and the perimeter offsets are measured from that rectangle, an interpretation that places some links further than s0 from the actual column faces (the outer rings are then not widened); this needs equal spacing both ways and a spacing ≤ 0.75d, otherwise the entered sr, st are used with a note; grid lines parallel to the column faces at the perimeter positions; across the column the lines start at the corners of the first ring (s0 outside the faces) and step inward at exactly st, with a closing line on the centre where needed; ring gaps are measured as straight distances between neighbouring links, default 150 mm; ring k holds the grid points on the rectangle s0 + k·sr from the faces; with wider spacing on outer rings (default on) every ring, the first included, keeps only every m-th grid point, m = 2, 3 … never decreasing outward, as long as the gap stays ≤ 1.5d inside u1 and ≤ 2d outside (so uout,ef keeps its full length), the ring still carries the Eq. 6.52 Asw and one leg meets Eq. 9.11 with that gap), radial rails (Fig. 6.22 A: the face rails of the cruciform layout plus m rails fanning out from each column corner at equal angles; a corner rail starts at the first perimeter where the corner gap without it would exceed the st limit or the face rails alone would not give the Eq. 6.52 Asw (near the column the corner studs would otherwise crowd round the corner); the tool takes the smallest m that keeps st ≤ 1.5d inside u1 — the corner gap is (π/2)·o/(m + 1) at offset o — and lets uout,ef reach uout,req; circular columns get equally spaced rays, from 8 up) and cruciform rails (Fig. 6.22 B: rails perpendicular to the faces only, the outer ones in line with the column edges, with enough between them to keep ≤ 1.5d along each face; when uout,ef falls short the tool adds the fewest parallel rails beside each arm at the same spacing, widening the arms). Constraints first. The user sets the link bar Ø, the maximum radial spacing sr and, for a grid off the bars, the maximum st; the algorithm works within them. Optimise sr (default): the tool tries the radial spacings the arrangement allows up to the entered maximum — on the bar grid every whole multiple of the bar spacing up to 0.75d, otherwise 0.75d rounded down to 5 mm and steps of 25 mm below it down to 0.4d — and keeps the one with the fewest links that passes every check (if none passes, the lowest UR); the choice is stated in a note. With auto sr off, the entered sr is capped at 0.75d rounded down to 5 mm (§9.4.3(1)) and, on the bar grid, rounded down to a whole multiple of the bar spacing. The bar Ø is the one entered; radial and cruciform layouts take the fewest rails that also give every perimeter the Asw of Eq. 6.52 at that sr, while the grid keeps the entered st and reports a shortfall (reduce st or use a larger bar). The arrangement is then checked as placed. Each perimeter is one layer for Eq. 6.52 and §9.4.3. No link is placed outside the slab, in an opening or in its shadow.

Required and effective outer perimeter (internal columns without openings or a drop). The required length uout,req = β·VEd/(vRd,c·d) (Eq. 6.54) depends only on the load and the concrete. The effective perimeter uout,ef depends on the arrangement: it runs 1.5d outside the polygon through the outermost links (Fig. 6.22 A), and where two adjacent outer links are more than 2d apart only d beyond each of them counts (Fig. 6.22 B). The tool takes uout,ef = Σ min(Li, 2d) + 2π·1.5d, Li the distances between adjacent outer links around the column — an interpretation of the figure, which gives no formula. The rails are extended until uout,ef ≥ uout,req; when gaps that count only 2d stop the perimeter from growing before the slab edge, the arrangement stays at the aout length and the outer check fails with a note giving the largest uout,ef the arrangement can reach. For cruciform rails of the column width that limit is 2(c1 + c2) + 8d + 3πd however long the rails are (the face gaps are fixed and each corner gap counts at most 2d); widening the arms raises it, which is why the tool adds rails beside the arms rather than lengthening them. The plan shows uout,req faint and uout,ef with its excluded parts left out.

Tangential spacing st (§9.4.3(1)). Inside u1 (≤ 2d from the face) st ≤ 1.5d is a check, measured along the control path at the layer (grid points beyond a column corner are projected onto the rounded path). For cruciform rails only the gaps between rails on the same face count: Fig. 6.22 B itself shows the gap around a column corner larger than 1.5d inside u1, and that region is covered by the reduced uout,ef (interpretation). Outside u1 a gap larger than 2d is not a failure: that part of the perimeter is excluded from uout,ef (§9.4.3(1): "where that part of the perimeter is assumed to contribute"), and Eq. 9.11 is evaluated with st = 2d for the legs that do count. At edge and corner columns, with openings or a drop, the outer perimeter stays the clipped path at aout and st ≤ 2d outside u1 remains a check. Auto designs saved before the arrangement choice reopen on the grid. With a drop (lH > 2hH) the rings inside the drop continue to within one sr of the drop edge, and on the grid further links on the same lines fill the drop out to its edge less cover on every side, at whole multiples of the grid step as for the outer rings (never denser than the last ring; ≤ 1.5d within 2d of the face, ≤ 2d beyond, both ways), so the outer zone of the drop is reinforced up to the depth change. These extra links are detailing: they are not part of any perimeter in Eq. 6.52 or §9.4.3.

Flexural reinforcement is entered as bar Ø and spacing in each direction. Designs saved with an area per metre open with the equivalent spacing for the stored bar Ø, so As is unchanged.

7 Shear stress figure

The stress figure shows vEd along u0, u1 and uout,ef (or u1,ext for a drop). The mean value is VEd/(u·d). When β comes from moments at an internal column (Eq. 6.39, 6.42 or 6.43) the fence is the fully plastic distribution of Fig. 6.19 that Eq. 6.39 is derived from: vEd(s) = vV ± Σ vM,i, with vV = VEd/(u·d) and, for each axis, vM,i = k·VEd·ei/(W1·d) added on the side the moment pushes down and removed on the other, a step at the centroid axis. W1 = ∫|x| dl is taken for the perimeter drawn (Eq. 6.40) and k from Table 6.1; a circular column takes one term about the resultant eccentricity with k = 0.6 (Eq. 6.42). For one moment the peak vV + vM at u1 equals β·vV of Eq. 6.39. For moments about both axes the tool checks with Eq. 6.43, so the sum of the two plastic terms can differ from β·vV; each panel prints both, and the check always uses β·vV. Fence height uses one scale for all panels; colour runs from blue at the lowest to red at the highest stress of each panel. With the simplified or a user β, and at edge and corner columns, the figure shows the uniform envelope β·VEd/(u·d). The check itself always uses β·VEd/(u·d).

8 Benchmarks against published examples

Each published example is entered with its own data and run through the tool. Same rule rows must agree within the rounding of the published value (test test/benchmarks.test.mjs); Different rule rows rest on another National Annex, another β method or a pre-A1:2014 text, and the reason is given. Reproduce with node tools/punching-shear/scripts/benchmarks.mjs.

Summary: every value computed under the same rule agrees within 0.1 % (vmin of EUR 26566 within its two-decimal rounding). The differences come from β for biaxial moments (CSI sums Eq. 6.39 over both axes, the tool uses Eq. 6.43), the sr rounding to 5 mm, the A1:2014 limit kmax·vRd,c and the German National Annex in the Dlubal examples. No published example with a column head, an opening or an edge or corner column has been found yet; those parts are checked against hand calculation only (reference cases in scripts/verification.mjs, listed in SOURCES.md).

Effective outer perimeter of a cruciform layout. Ancon Shearfix Punching Shear Reinforcement, EC2 Design Manual, Example 16.1 (internal 600 × 400 column, d = 204 mm, outer stud lines 804 and 604 mm wide) gives uout,ef = 2(ysr0 + 2d + 0.75πd + zsr0 + 2d + 0.75πd) = 6371 mm. The tool's rule Σ min(Li, 2d) + 2π·1.5d gives 6371 mm for the same outer studs (test/arrangement.test.mjs), so the interpretation of Fig. 6.22 B agrees with the manufacturer's procedure. Only the perimeter formula is compared: the manual's rail layout (main and secondary rails, 3 studs per rail) and its uout,req inputs are not re-run. The manual also notes that more than three studs per rail do not increase uout,ef, as found here.

9 How mPunchingShear calculates results

Result status. Pass and Fail show the outcome of the applicable checks against the selected design basis. Software methods such as rectangular-head sector mapping are identified alongside the result. Incomplete input and Unsupported geometry or loading identify conditions outside the calculation. Links required indicates that the concrete demand exceeds its resistance.

Singapore National Annex. The face resistance uses 0.5νfcd, and the upper resistance with links uses 2.0vRd,c. Where the required outer control perimeter is closer than 3d to the loaded area, place links in the zone from 0.3d to 1.5d, with the first layer no further out than 0.5d. The zone checks use each link leg’s shortest distance to the loaded area, including at corners.

Defaults. New designs use the Singapore National Annex, β from moments and effective depths derived from the slab thickness, cover and flexural bar layers. Punching links are off when a design starts. When enabled, the default arrangement is an orthogonal grid with spacing inputs of 150 mm.
