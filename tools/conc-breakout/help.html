mConcBreakout · Calculation Help

Cast-in steel plate with welded reinforcing bars and headed studs, loaded by NEd, VEd and MEd in one plane. Tags show the basis of each step: Code a direct code rule, Method a published method adapted here, Assumption a modelling choice by this tool.

1 Coordinates and sign convention

The plate face lies in the x–y plane with the origin at the plate centre; y points up and z points into the concrete. NEd > 0 pulls the plate out. VEd acts downward at eV from the plate face. The total moment MEd + VEd·eV must put the top (+y) in tension. Edge distances are measured from the plate centre to each free edge of the receiving member; a blank edge is far away. hef is measured from the concrete face, so a flush plate adds its thickness to the stud length under the plate.

2 Load path and equilibrium

Assumption The plate is rigid. Tension elements strain linearly with their distance from the compression centre and carry force in proportion to area × distance. That is how moment tension is shared between several bar rows, and between bars and studs when studs share tension.

Code Flange T-stub model (default): bearing acts under the compressed flange over beff × leff, extended by c = t·√(fy/(3fjdγM0)), with its centre at the centroid of that area. Source: EN 1993-1-8 §6.2.5, Eq. 6.5–6.6. fjd = βj·α·fcd, where α ≤ 3 follows EN 1992-1-1 Eq. 6.63 and the engineer must justify it from the spread geometry.

Assumption Full-width block: fjd over the full plate width from the bottom edge. The tool warns when B exceeds b + 2c, because the plate cannot then spread bearing across its full width.

The lever arm is not entered. It comes out of equilibrium: ΣT − C = N and ΣT·y − C·yc = M. With pure tension at small eccentricity, a linear field over the tension elements is used with no bearing.

2b Flexible plate (FEM), the default analysis

Assumption The plate is modelled with MITC4 Reissner–Mindlin plate elements on a mesh of at most 25 mm. Grid lines pass through every bar, stud and flange line. The steel member is a rigid footprint over both flanges and the web line, an I-section, and carries NEd and MEd. Shear stays with the studs as in the rigid model.

  • Concrete: compression-only Winkler springs, kc = Ecm/(1.275·√(B·H)) per unit area. This is the concrete-in-compression coefficient k13 of EN 1993-1-8 Table 6.11 spread over the plate. Ecm is from EN 1992-1-1 Table 3.1.
  • Welded bars: tension-only springs EsAs/le, with le = ½·min(lb,prov, lb,rqd), spread over the nodes within the weld collar or one mesh step.
  • Studs, when they share tension: tension-only springs EsAs/hef, the steel shank only. This is stiffer than the real stud, so stud tension is not underestimated.
  • When studs share tension, the stud springs are an upper bound on stud stiffness and would unload the bars. The model is therefore solved twice. The bars take the larger force from a run without stud tension and the run with it, and the studs take their force from the run with stiff stud springs. Each component is checked for its upper bound.
  • One-sided springs are solved by active-set iteration. The bar and stud forces from FEM replace the rigid-plate forces in every check. The bearing check compares the FEM compression resultant with the flange T-stub Fc,Rd.
  • The plate is elastic. The von Mises stress at element centres is reported against fy/γM0 as a warning, not a failure. Peaks next to point springs depend on the mesh, and the yield-line strip check stays the resistance check. No plastic redistribution (EN 1993-1-5 Annex C) is modelled.
  • Verification (test/fem.test.mjs): Navier simply supported plate under a point load and under a uniform load (Timoshenko coefficients) within 1–1.5%. A 400 mm thick plate reproduces an independent rigid-plate-on-Winkler solution within 2%. Global equilibrium is checked for N < 0, = 0 and > 0.

What changes physically: a thin plate concentrates bearing under the compression flange and raises the bar force. A thick plate behaves rigidly on the elastic concrete, so bearing moves toward the plate edge and the lever arm grows. The "Flexible vs rigid plate" row reports both.

3 Welded bars

  • Code Steel: F ≤ As·fyk/γs.
  • Code Fillet weld all round the bar: EN 1993-1-8 §4.5.3.3, using fu = min(plate fu, k·fyk), weld length πØ. The welding procedure, joint type and bar weldability must follow EN ISO 17660-1, which this tool does not check. A warning appears when the weld is weaker than the bar's design yield.
  • Code Anchorage: EN 1992-1-1 §8.4 with the actual bar stress, α1 = 0.7 for bent bars with cd > 3Ø, α2…α5 = 1.0. lb,prov is measured along the bar from the plate. Mandrel: Table 8.1N. Bearing stress inside the bend is not checked.
  • Method Plate shear around the weld toe: perimeter π(Ø + 2√2·a) × t × fy/√3.

4 Plate bending

Method Each bar row loads a strip cantilevering from the toe of the member weld on the tension flange, m = yrow − hm/2 − 0.8√2·aw. The strip width leff is the smaller of the circular and non-circular yield-line lengths of EN 1993-1-8 Table 6.4, using the end-row group patterns, limited to the plate width. FRd = Mpl,Rd/m. There is no prying amplification. That assumes the plate beyond the bar row does not bear on the concrete, which differs from bolted base plates: a bar bonded along its length is much stiffer than a holding-down bolt. Rows inside the member outline (two-way bending between walls) are reported as not covered.

Assumption Rigid-plate indicator: plate cantilever stiffness 3EI/m³ against bar axial stiffness EA/le, where le = ½·min(lb,prov, lb,rqd). Below 10, plate bending changes the force split between rows and between bars and studs. Check those cases with a plate (shell) FE model. This is an engineering criterion, not a code rule.

6 Answers to the key modelling questions

Can the concrete cone be omitted because welded bars carry the tension?

For the studs, only when the studs really are free of tension: they must sit in the compression zone, or the bars must be much stiffer so that plate deformation does not load them. The tool flags any stud in the tension zone when stud tension is neglected. The bars themselves are not anchors under EN 1992-4. Their tension must be anchored beyond any potential breakout body and lapped into the member reinforcement. That is the separately confirmed load path, not supplementary reinforcement in the sense of §7.2.1.9.

When can studs be designed for shear only?

When the rigid-plate strain field puts them at or below the compression centre, or when a flexible-plate analysis shows that their tension is negligible. Otherwise select "Studs share tension".

Where is the compression resultant, and what is the lever arm?

The compression resultant sits at the centroid of the effective bearing area under the compressed flange (T-stub model), or at x/2 for the full-width block. The lever arm is the distance from it to the tension resultant, which comes from equilibrium.

Strut-and-tie model of the receiving member

The section drawing shows a conceptual D-region and strut lines; it is not a solved STM. The tool checks the bearing node stress against k1ν′fcd (EN 1992-1-1 Eq. 6.60), but a statically admissible STM depends on the member's own reinforcement, which is not an input. The design stays Incomplete until the engineer confirms that the bar ties, the bearing strut and any breakout bodies are carried into the member reinforcement.

Is the SCI / EN 1993-1-8 T-stub directly applicable?

Its yield-line lengths are, because they depend on the plate and the load points, not on the fastener type. Its prying and bolt-elongation rules are not. Welded bonded bars are stiff and cannot separate from the plate, which is why the strip check here uses a cantilever with no prying.

7 Drawing convention and 3D view

The 3D tab is an SVG projection: drag to rotate, use the wheel or buttons to zoom, or the arrow keys. It colours the plate by σvM, deflection w or contact pressure, with optional exaggerated deformation, and shows bars coloured by utilisation and the checked breakout bodies dashed. 8 Not covered

  • Supplementary reinforcement for stud breakout, blow-out values, and splitting from product data.
  • The steel member, the member-to-plate weld, and two-way plate bending inside the member outline.
